Research series

The Mechanics of Macroscopic Electrodynamics

More than a century after Abraham and Minkowski, macroscopic electrodynamics still offers competing answers to a basic question: what energy and momentum do the fields carry inside matter? This five-paper series proposes a resolution by treating electrodynamics, continuum mechanics and thermodynamics as three descriptions of one physical system.

The papers

The series constructs a common account of what fields and matter store, what they transport, and how they exchange energy and momentum, built from the same charged constituents and microscopic fields.

  1. I Published

    Same macroscopic fields, different local mechanical loads: an electromechanical closure diagnostic

    Which force densities and boundary tractions drive deformation? Controlled comparisons show that identical macroscopic fields can accompany different local mechanical loads, and establish when the macroscopic description is sufficient.

    Phys. Rev. Applied 26, 054011 (2026) · open access

  2. II Manuscript

    Energy Architecture and Mechanical Closure in Macroscopic Dielectrics

    A common energy map for thermodynamics, continuum mechanics and macroscopic electrodynamics, tracing their energy stores to the same charged constituents and microscopic fields.

  3. III Manuscript

    A Mechanical Resolution of the EM Energy–Momentum Tensor Debate

    Requiring consistency between force and work through averaging identifies energy density, momentum density, energy flux and stress jointly, and argues for the vacuum-form tensor of the averaged fields, including inside matter.

  4. IV In preparation

    Magnetic matter

    Extends the account to magnetic matter with effective current models, including the work needed to maintain persistent magnetic moments.

  5. V In preparation

    Current and magnetic-charge models

    Compares effective current and magnetic-charge models: the same selected forces and work under specified conditions, but a different assignment of energy.
